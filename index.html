SWAB · Event Day Explorer

We only get one shot at event day each year.

Hundreds of kids, mentors and parents go through check-in, matching, shopping and checkout in a single day. Once it's over, we usually only have what people remember.

2026 was the first year we ran event day on our own software. Every check-in, match and pickup was saved as it happened, so now we can go back and see how the day actually went.

Each year we'll add the new event day here, so we can plan the next one off real numbers.

What 2026 showed us

A few things you can see straight away. The explorer has plenty more.

9 min
Typical wait for a kid to get a group

At 9 am, half the kids had a group within 5 minutes. By 10 am only 16% did, and the line peaked at 63 kids at 11:51.

19 min
Typical wait for a mentor pair to get a kid

Kids and mentors took turns waiting. Mentors spent 164 hours waiting in total, kids 73.

6 min
Typical checkout, from text to release

The worst line (27 families at 1:07) was parents waiting for groups to walk back. Texting mentors sooner would help more than extra laptops.

3.8 hrs
Typical time a kid was out shopping

From getting a group to going home. 417 of the 423 kids have a recorded home time.

118
Times someone had to step in

41 kids typed in by hand, 51 mentor fixes, 24 pickup edge cases and 2 phones added.

1.37
Mentors per kid at noon

Mentor shifts came in waves, so groups formed in bursts instead of a steady flow.

How to get around

  1. Board or StoryBoard puts every chart on one page. Story walks you through the day one step at a time with Back and Next. Switch between them in the bottom right.
  2. Play with the clockThe bar at the bottom runs the day. Press Play, change the speed, or drag to any time. The orange marks jump to the big moments. Every chart moves with it.
  3. Hover anythingEvery dial, dot and line tells you what it is when you hover over it.
  4. Click to dig inClick a kid, group or family and it lights up in every chart. Click a dial for more detail. Esc to go back.
  5. Hit i when you're not sureEvery chart has an i in its corner. It explains how to read it and what's worth noticing, with times you can click to jump to.
